## 2. The nave

The main body of the church, where the congregation gathers, is called the nave. Everything in it leads the eye forward: the aisle, the pews and the lights all point to the carved icon screen at the front and the altar behind it. Church tradition compares the building to a ship, like Noah's ark, that carries its people safely.

## 4. Stained glass: the New Testament side

The windows on the right wall show events from the life of Christ as the Gospels tell them. Together they show who Christians believe he is: the one who has power over nature, sickness and death, and who comes looking for every person. Choose a window to read its story.

### Christ calms the storm

`Win_storm`

A storm rose while Christ and his disciples were crossing the lake, and the disciples feared they would drown. He spoke to the wind and the waves and they became still (Mark 4). The disciples asked one another, "Who is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"

### The Samaritan woman at the well

`Win_samaritan`

Christ asked a Samaritan woman for a drink at a well and then offered her living water, the life that comes from God (John 4). She went and told her whole town about him.

When King Herod sought to kill the child Jesus, Joseph took him and his mother Mary to Egypt, where they stayed until Herod died (Matthew 2). Copts treasure this journey, because their country sheltered the Holy Family. The Coptic Church keeps a feast for it on the first of June.

Lazarus, a friend of Christ, had been dead for four days when Christ called him out of the tomb alive (John 11). The Church remembers it on Lazarus Saturday, the day before Palm Sunday, as a sign of the resurrection to come.

## 5. Feast icons on the walls

Carved wooden shrines on the walls hold icons. Those between the windows show great feasts of the church year, each one an event in the story of salvation: the Annunciation, the Nativity, the Resurrection, the Ascension and Pentecost. With them, and near the back of the church, are saints who are especially loved in the Coptic Church. An icon is honoured, never worshipped; it is there to turn the mind toward the person or event it shows.

### The Annunciation

`Icon_annunciation`

The Archangel Gabriel tells the Virgin Mary that she will bear the Son of God, and she accepts (Luke 1). The feast celebrates the moment God became man.

### The Nativity

`Icon_nativity`

The birth of Christ in Bethlehem (Luke 2). The Coptic Church celebrates Christmas on the 29th of the Coptic month of Kiahk, which falls on the 7th of January.

### The Resurrection

`Icon_resurrection`

Christ rises from the dead on the third day after his crucifixion. Easter, the feast of the Resurrection, is the greatest feast of the Church, and every Sunday is kept in its memory.

### The Ascension

`Icon_ascension`

Forty days after his Resurrection, Christ was taken up into heaven while his disciples watched (Acts 1).

### Pentecost

`Icon_pentecost`

Fifty days after the Resurrection, the Holy Spirit came down on the disciples in the form of tongues of fire, and they began to preach to every nation (Acts 2). It is kept as the birthday of the Church.

### St. Antony the Great

`Icon_antony`

St. Antony was an Egyptian who gave away what he owned and went to live a life of prayer in the desert in the third and fourth centuries. Others followed him, and he is known as the father of monks.

### St. Mina

`Icon_mina`

St. Mina was an Egyptian soldier who was martyred for his faith in the early fourth century. He is called the Wonderworker and is one of the best loved saints of Egypt. His monastery stands at Mariout, near Alexandria.

### Abba Abraam

`Icon_abraam`

Abba Abraam was Bishop of Fayoum in Egypt and died in 1914. He is remembered as the friend of the poor, because he gave away whatever he was given.
